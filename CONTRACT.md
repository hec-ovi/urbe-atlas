# CONTRACT: atlas

Purpose: deterministically generates the 2D city blueprint (districts, streets with sidewalks, typed parcels with 3D envelopes, transit and optional hydrology) from a seed and parameters.

Status: package v0.4.0, blueprint v0.17.0. Breaking changes go through the orchestrator.

## Conventions
- Units: meters. Ground plane XZ, +Y up. 2D points are `[x, z]`; heights along +Y.
- Polygons: CCW rings, first point not repeated.
- Determinism: same seed + params, byte-identical blueprint JSON.
- IDs: deterministic strings with a disjoint prefix per collection: `d` district, `n` street node, `e` street edge, `b` block, `p` parcel, `bs` bus stop, `br` bus route, `ts` train station, `tl` train line, `ss` subway station, `sl` subway line, `w` water body, `sh` shoreline and `ws` water structure.

## In
`generateCity(params: AtlasParams): CityBlueprint`

Params: [schema/params.ts](schema/params.ts). Only `seed` is required; every other field has a documented default (size, irregularity, district count range, max floors global and per district kind, wealth tier weights, feature toggles for highways, trains, subways, alleys, air and underground tunnels). `hydrology: { type }` optionally selects `lagoon`, `river`, or `sea-coast`; omission means no water and adds no output field.
`streetDesign` supplies numeric road and sidewalk profiles, [src/streets/construction/schema/design.ts](src/streets/construction/schema/design.ts). Omission selects 7, 14 and 21 m ground roadways and 3, 4.5, 6.5 and 8.5 m sidewalk profiles. Profiles are selected before land subdivision.
`footprintShape` defaults to `rectangle`; explicit `parcel` follows the setback-adjusted lot shape. Rectangles use the shared half-metre construction grid and the same hosting policy for use eligibility and final buildability.
Nested parameter objects and arrays are validated at runtime. Unknown district kinds, wealth tiers and feature names, non-boolean toggles, malformed ranges and non-finite numbers fail with `E_INVALID_PARAMS`. Browser parameter imports apply the same validation before changing the form.

CLI: after `npm run build:cli`, `npm run generate -- --seed <seed> --out <file.json> [--size N] [--irregularity X] [--max-floors N] [--no-highways] [--no-trains] [--no-subways] [--no-alleys]` executes the prepared artifact without modifying the Atlas package and writes the blueprint JSON only to the requested path. Exit 1 on AtlasError (code printed), 2 on usage error. `npm run preview` prepares the same CLI before its server starts.

Build: `npm run build` type-checks the package, writes the reusable CLI to `dist/cli.mjs`, and writes the production browser preview under `dist/preview/`. `npm run build:cli` builds only the CLI. Building either surface preserves the other.

Samples, committed and test-guaranteed to regenerate byte-identical:
- [samples/city-urbe.json](samples/city-urbe.json): seed `urbe`, default params (full-size city).
- [samples/city-urbe-small.json](samples/city-urbe-small.json): seed `urbe-small`, size 800x800 (village, first complete-city build target).
- [samples/city-urbe-tiny.json](samples/city-urbe-tiny.json): seed `urbe-tiny`, size 400x400, maxFloors 6, highways, trains and subways off (smallest coherent city).

## Out
`CityBlueprint`: [schema/blueprint.ts](schema/blueprint.ts).
- `meta`: schema version, seed, resolved params, bounds, city grid angle, irregular city boundary polygon and `buildingGrid: {origin: [0,0], angle: gridAngle, spacing: 0.5}`. Grid angle is radians; coordinates and spacing are metres.
- `districts`: kind (downtown, commercial, residential, industrial, mixed), wealth tier, boundary (a rectangle on the city grid, clipped to the city outline), floor cap.
- `streets`: planar graph of nodes and edges; each edge has class (`street` | `road` | `highway` | `alley`), centerline path (curves as polylines), carriageway width, per-side sidewalk widths, and an `elevationProfile` of `{ distance, level }` knots measured along its own path. `level` remains the flat or maximum height (0 at grade, 8 on a highway deck, [src/levels.ts](src/levels.ts)). Node `connections` group incident edges by their endpoint level, and a vehicle transfers only inside one group, so a grade road does not connect to the highway crossing above it. The graph also carries pedestrian crossings. Each crossing segment names its street edge, connects the two sidewalk walking bands, has a 3 m width, and publishes fitted 0.5 m zebra stripe polygons over the carriageway. Traffic signals have one head per junction arm with the mast that carries it. `planting` is one flat list of street furniture points for the whole city, each with its `edgeId`, its `kind` (`tree` | `pole` | `bin`, a closed set: skip a kind you do not know rather than draw it wrong) and the spacing its stretch runs at. `highwayStructures` has one construction record per maximal highway run with its ordered edge IDs, continuous path, width, level, deck thickness, ramp lengths, matching elevation profile and support columns. An alley is pedestrian only: carriageway 0, 3 to 5 m of ground, all of it sidewalk, cut through long blocks (dense in poor and commercial districts, elsewhere only as a mid-block connector).
- `blocks`: street-bounded faces with a curb strip, sidewalk strip polygons, contained parcels, open areas. Curb corners at intersections carry a rounded return.
- `streets.construction`: ordered through-runs with stable `sr` ids and path distances, [src/streets/construction/schema/sections.ts](src/streets/construction/schema/sections.ts). Grade edges publish `crossSection`: exact lane offsets and separate left/right curb, border, furnishing, walking and frontage bands. Scalar widths equal these totals. Highway dimensions retain their structure contract. Crossing `roadway` endpoints describe the marking span separately from unequal pedestrian approaches.
- `streets.construction.planningReservations`: exact edge-local roadway, left/right sidewalk and walking polygons with their directed sweep model, [src/streets/construction/corridors/CONTRACT.md](src/streets/construction/corridors/CONTRACT.md). These planning queries include bends and caps. Final ground polygons apply junction, elevation and water ownership; a reservation is not an alternate render or collision surface.
- `parcels`: type (residential, hotel, offices, corpo, hospital, clinic, police, military, factory, commerce, mall, restaurant, coffee_shop), tier (poor, mid, rich, high_rich), lot polygon, footprint polygon, street access point and 3D envelope (min/max floors, nominal floor height and max height; real per-floor elevations are owned by Exterior). A default footprint is a complete grid-aligned rectangle inside its setback land. It satisfies the type's minimum band and core fit; unused land remains part of its lot. Explicit `parcel` footprints follow the inset lot and required band.
- `transit`: bus stops and routes over street edges; train and subway stations and lines, each with its `level` (trains at grade, subways at -12 m) and full corridor `width` (4 m train bed, 6 m subway tunnel diameter). Rail paths advance without an immediate reciprocal segment. A station publishes its platform polygon and vertical box (5 m headroom underground, 3 m at grade), street-level entrances, and underground shafts and `accessPaths`. Subway `entranceBays` name each directed street side, full reserved land, shaft and walking-band approach, [src/transit/reservations/schema.ts](src/transit/reservations/schema.ts). `subwayDemand` records the pre-parcel population forecast and line target. Each line ends inside its terminal platforms. An access path is ordered `[x, y, z]` centerline data: switchback stairs inside the shaft, then a level passage to the platform. Grade stations publish neither shafts nor access paths.
- `hydrology` (optional): [src/hydro/schema/hydrology-plan.schema.json](src/hydro/schema/hydrology-plan.schema.json). It publishes independent `seedId`, water surfaces, shoreline paths and water-side construction bands, elevation, depth, material key and bridge/tunnel contacts. A structure's optional `corridor` is its exact wet-contact reservation; `path` locates that contact along the source network and `width` remains metadata. The source network owns the complete deck or tunnel geometry. Renderers consume water `surfaces` at `elevation`, shade to `depth`, resolve `materialKey`, and draw shoreline `path` and `band`.
- `volumetric`: low poly city for map previews: one prism per parcel plus land cover polygons (roadway, curb, sidewalk, block, open). Every ground polygon publishes its construction `bottom` and walkable `top`: roadway -0.2 to 0 m, curb 0 to 0.15 m, and sidewalk, block and open ground 0 to 0.15 m. When hydrology exists, these polygons cover land only; water surfaces are separate.
- `stats`: population estimate, parcel counts per type, per district.

## Errors
Closed set, thrown as `AtlasError { code, message, details? }` ([schema/blueprint.ts](schema/blueprint.ts)):
- `E_INVALID_PARAMS`: params fail validation; message names the field.
- `E_UNSATISFIABLE`: params cannot yield a coherent city (e.g. size too small for the district count, or below 420 m on one axis when hydrology is requested).
- `E_INVARIANT`: post-generation coherence check failed; atlas bug, report with seed and params.

## Invariants
- Rectangular hosting maximizes whole-cell area, then short-side width, then lower grid coordinates and wider U dimension. Full transformed lattice coordinates preserve each building corner; analytic containment checks the complete rectangle against its setback land, allowing only floating-point transform roundoff. Ineligible lots use the existing lighter use, neighbor merge or open-land policy; no rectangle is clipped into an irregular building.
- Bus stops keep the researched spacing in a city 1.6 km across or larger and close in proportionally below that, so a small city still runs routes. Route search uses node and endpoint level as one state. Every consecutive edge pair shares a node and belongs to the same `connections` group at that node, so a bus cannot transfer between a grade road and the highway deck above it.
- Planar streets: when the traced network leaves two faces sharing ground, the streets grow again from the next seed in line (three tries), and a city that never planarizes refuses with `E_UNSATISFIABLE`. Alleys are cut into that network only while they keep it planar; a set of alleys that would leave two faces sharing ground is dropped and the city keeps its blocks whole.
- Street pattern: every gridded district shares one city grid angle, so blocks stay square and monotonous across districts. The tensor field applies no random noise rotation. A downtown turns radial only when `irregularity` is 0.4 or more, and the boundary bends streets only in proportion to `irregularity`. Default size is 1000 x 1000 m and default irregularity is 0.35; 0.4 and up rings the downtown.
- Street placement reserves the widest eligible half-corridor inside the city boundary before tracing, [src/streets/domain/CONTRACT.md](src/streets/domain/CONTRACT.md). Complete tracer steps, joins and graph edits stay inside that domain; published per-side widths remain inside city land. An empty or disconnected domain fails with `E_UNSATISFIABLE`.
- District shape follows that same grid: the planned centers are halved by cuts perpendicular to a grid axis, so a district is a rectangle in grid space clipped to the city outline. `irregularity` is the only thing that leaves the grid: it slides a cut off the midpoint by up to a quarter of the gap it splits and leans it by up to 15 degrees, both in proportion. At irregularity 0 every cut is exactly on the grid.
- Levels are the one place height lives for networks: a consumer reads street height by linearly interpolating its ordered elevation profile, and stacks flat tracks and platforms from `level`, never from class. Every street profile starts at distance 0, ends at the exact 2D path length, has strictly increasing distances and stays within 0 and its declared maximum level. Every non-highway profile is flat. Node connection groups contain every incident edge exactly once and only join equal endpoint levels.
- A highway is a through route: every highway chain ends either at a junction with other highway arms or within 30 m of the city boundary, so a deck never dead-ends over a block. A chain that stops inside the city or cannot hold its ramps plus a supported flat span is demoted to `road` before widths and blocks are read. When tracing leaves no usable highway, the shortest graph route between the farthest city-edge nodes becomes one complete route before blocks and parcels are derived.
- Every highway edge belongs to exactly one `streets.highwayStructures` record. Its ramps rise linearly from grade to the 8 m deck over 60 m only at city-edge termini, shortened only when the whole run is shorter. The structure and its routing edges publish the same height knots, including a breakpoint when a ramp ends inside an edge. Its 2 x 2 m supports stand under the flat deck at no more than 30 m intervals, from ground to the deck underside; a support moves across the deck or shifts the pitch backward where grade infrastructure crosses it. Each shift is bounded by the preceding support, so placement always advances or fails with `E_INVARIANT`. Decks keep 1 m of construction clearance beyond each edge from every building footprint. Support footprints never enter a building footprint, grade rail corridor, subway entrance shaft, curb or sidewalk. All support centers are on the 1 mm coordinate grid.
- Highway construction supports an early envelope stage and later support placement, [src/streets/construction/highway/CONTRACT.md](src/streets/construction/highway/CONTRACT.md). The edge-only envelope contains every final structure field except supports. Support completion preserves that envelope exactly; the combined entry point remains available. Assigned routing profiles are checked against the shared run height function.
- IDs are globally unique across the whole blueprint (disjoint prefixes per collection).
- Street graph is connected; every parcel's access point lies on a sidewalk of its access edge.
- Every edge is a real run of street: its two nodes differ, no path point repeats, and no vertex turns more than 120 degrees. A sharper turn would fold the edge's sidewalk band back over its own roadway.
- A physical street path appears once per source family, independent of direction or collinear subdivisions on the coordinate grid. Street and road traces share a family with road precedence; highways and pedestrian alleys have separate families.
- Initial graph normalization resolves same-endpoint, same-family paths at the configured node snap radius. Both paths must advance strictly along their common endpoint chord and stay within that radius at every shared projected station. Returning paths and separated parallels remain distinct. Canonical order is class precedence, shorter length, then whole-path grid key; each removed path matches a retained representative directly. Extension uses exact identity only.
- `StreetGraphBuilder.extend(existingGraph, newLines, {domain})`, [graph types](src/streets/Graph.ts), adds traced paths to the current graph without simplification or radius clustering. Existing coordinates remain fixed. A source endpoint nodes a target segment only when that segment visits the endpoint's half-open 1 mm grid cell; both paths share the existing endpoint coordinate. Graph normalization and stable ID assignment run over the combined network.
- Initial graph simplification retains every source endpoint shared with another source path as a required anchor. Only exact shared coordinates establish these authored joins.
- Sidewalks are continuous along every street and road, linked across intersections by crossings. Each crossing segment references one incident street or road arm and publishes simple marking polygons spanning the carriageway. The 0.5 m bands repeat inside the 3 m crossing width with equal margins, so neither edge is cropped.
- Traffic signals are junction-wide: a junction where three or more streets meet at grade and at least one is a road carries one head on every arm, or none. Highways and pedestrian alleys carry no head. A pole stands in its approach's furnishing band (right-hand traffic); `facing` looks back down the arm and `mast` reaches square to it across the approach lanes to the roadway centerline. Crossings and signals use the actual widths of all incident arms.
- Street furniture stands in the kerb-side furnishing strip of a sidewalk, never in the walking line: 8 m apart in downtown and commercial districts, 12 m elsewhere ([src/streets/Planting.ts](src/streets/Planting.ts)), a light pole every third point, and always 6 m clear of a crossing, a bus stop, a station entrance and a parcel access point. Streets and roads carry it; an alley has no kerb and a highway no sidewalk.
- An alley has no carriageway, a sidewalk on both sides summing to 3 to 5 m (the bands of the two blocks it separates, which meet at its centerline), and carries no bus stop and no bus route: vehicles never enter one.
- Every stop and station belongs to a route or line serving at least two stops or stations. Rail networks are connected and contain no immediate double-back. Subway endpoints lie inside their terminal platforms; an insufficient route or unavailable entrance land fails with `E_UNSATISFIABLE`.
- Grade rail and subway entrance bays reserve land before parcels. Train beds and platforms keep 1 m of building clearance; platforms also avoid the highway deck corridor. Each subway shaft is 8 by 3 m with a 1 m apron outside the complete sidewalk. Its approach starts in the authored walking band, whose width remains clear. Bay footprints receive sidewalk paving and exclude parcel lots. The early subway plan uses the zoning population forecast and survives final population changes unchanged. Grade infrastructure and subway shafts stay clear of highway supports.
- A station is walkable end to end: its platform is a simple ring covering the station's position; underground it carries one shaft and one access path per entrance. The stair centerline descends through four non-vertical switchback flights inside its shaft, its passage stays at platform level, every leg joins exactly, and the final handoff lies inside the platform. An underground entrance is at most 30 m from its platform: NFPA 130 caps the walk from the platform's most remote point to the street at 100 m, and half a 140 m platform takes 70 of them. Platform sizes are researched ([src/transit/stations.ts](src/transit/stations.ts)): 140 x 8 m metro island, 180 x 6 m regional.
- Parcels never overlap; parcels, sidewalk and open areas cover their block; ground surfaces cover the city without gaps.
- The volumetric ground cover is a partition: roadway, curb, sidewalk, block and open polygons are pairwise disjoint. Coordinates live on a 1 mm grid, so surfaces sharing a boundary may report a sliver there; nothing overlaps by a band 1 cm or wider.
- Roadway covers every full-width street corridor on city land, including outer edges and junctions whose faces cannot host blocks. Every block clips against the complete network. Road-facing corner returns share that roadway ownership; remaining land is open ground.
- The curb is the outer 0.15 m of the block, between roadway and sidewalk ([src/streets/widths.ts](src/streets/widths.ts)). The curb follows the block boundary; the buildable interior excludes each street's complete left and right reservations. Short-edge cleanup only removes land and preserves alley source lines and pedestrian widths. Curb fragments below a 0.5 m run become sidewalk. Alley halves share one raised pedestrian seam; curb appears only at their roadway frontage. The per-edge sidewalk width includes its curb. Non-block street frontages receive the same reserved pedestrian ground.
- Block outlines and sidewalk polygons are simple rings (3+ points, real area, no crossing edges). Convex road-facing curb corners with room for a 0.6 m return use arcs of 1.5 to 3 m. Shared pedestrian seams keep their exact source boundary.
- Feature toggles are respected: a disabled feature produces no entities of that kind.
- Hydrology is derived from its independent seed stream before infrastructure placement. Water surfaces and shorelines are bounded, snapped to 1 mm, CCW, simple and byte-stable. Parcels, accesses, stations, entrances and land cover avoid water. A network touches water only inside its typed bridge or tunnel contact; constructed street contacts include both actual sidewalk widths. Highway supports in water belong to a published highway bridge.
- Every parcel footprint hosts the core rectangle its type needs, derived from interior's published core feasibility ([src/zoning/core.ts](src/zoning/core.ts) mirrors the constants of `../interior/schemas/core-feasibility.json`; a test fails when that file moves). A rectangle is a core mode's band length by its plate depth, with the stair shaft sized for the longest flight the recipe allows, plus one 0.5 m snap (the corridor face and the core start land on interior's grid) and twice the deepest facade (0.62 m) on both axes; it fits in either orientation. Walkup 11.14 x 9.74 m, walkup with two stairs 17.64 x 9.74 m, compact elevator core 12.14 x 13.74 m, standard elevator core 20.14 x 9.74 m. A heavy type (offices, corpo, hotel, hospital, mall, factory) hosts the compact rectangle; a light type (residential, commerce, restaurant, coffee_shop, clinic, police, military) hosts the walkup rectangle, and one of the two-stair rectangles when its footprint exceeds 460 m2.
- Every parcel footprint keeps its type's band end to end, the short side of its rectangle: `HEAVY_BAND` 12.14 m, `LIGHT_BAND` 9.74 m ([src/zoning/bands.ts](src/zoning/bands.ts)). The band is the width between the footprint's two long sides along the whole footprint, where an oblique end cut is a cap and not a narrowing. The zoner assigns a lot only types whose band it hosts; a heavy type whose footprint cannot host its rectangle is retyped to the district's main light type (commerce in downtown, commercial and industrial districts, residential in residential and mixed ones); a lot hosting no rectangle merges into a neighbour parcel or becomes open area.
- Envelope floors stay within what the hosted core allows: no cap with an elevator rectangle, 6 floors with the two-stair walkup rectangle, 4 with the walkup rectangle alone.
- Every envelope admits at least one floor at the minimum floor height of its type's family, mirrored from exterior's floor constants: 2.6 residential, 2.8 hotel, 3.4 offices, 3.6 corpo, 3.8 hospital and clinic, 3.0 police and military, 4.5 factory, 3.0 commerce, mall, restaurant and coffee shop.

## Depends on

- [Interior](../interior/CONTRACT.md): the mirrored [core-feasibility constants](../interior/schemas/core-feasibility.json) constrain parcel footprints and floor caps.
- [Exterior](../exterior/CONTRACT.md): the mirrored [floor constants](../exterior/schemas/floor-constants.json) constrain envelope floor heights.

Atlas imports no sibling runtime data. These compatibility edges keep every generated parcel buildable by the published building contracts.
