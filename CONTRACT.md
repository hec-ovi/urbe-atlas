# CONTRACT: atlas

Purpose: deterministically generates the 2D city blueprint (districts, streets with sidewalks, typed parcels with 3D envelopes, transit and optional hydrology) from a seed and parameters.

Status: package v0.12.5, blueprint v0.26.0, architecture 1.0.0. Breaking changes go through the orchestrator.

## Conventions
- Units: meters. Ground plane XZ, +Y up. 2D points are `[x, z]`; heights along +Y.
- Polygons: CCW rings, first point not repeated. The plan is rectangles: every ring in the land plan and the ground cover is four axis-aligned corners, checked before the blueprint is returned. A city with `hydrology` is the exception, where water cuts land along its shoreline.
- Determinism: same seed + params, byte-identical blueprint JSON.
- IDs: deterministic strings with a disjoint prefix per collection: `d` district, `n` street node, `e` street edge, `b` block, `p` parcel, `ss` subway station, `sl` subway line, `w` water body, `sh` shoreline and `ws` water structure.

## In
`generateCity(params: AtlasParams, onProgress?: ProgressObserver): CityBlueprint`

The optional observer receives completed pipeline stages and the current phase, [schema/progress.ts](schema/progress.ts). `GENERATION_STAGES` publishes the pipeline in order: each stage's stable id, the phase label reported while it runs, and a plain sentence naming what it leaves behind. A generated run reports every stage once, in that order, with `completed` equal to the stages finished before it; the last stage, writing the blueprint, completes when the caller has stored it. `GENERATION_RESULT` states in the same plain words what a finished blueprint carries and what it leaves to the stages that read it. Counts measure completed stages, not elapsed time. The blueprint and deterministic geometry are unchanged by observation.

Params: [schema/params.ts](schema/params.ts). Only `seed` is required; every other field has a documented default (size, default 3000 x 3000 m, district count range, max floors global and per district kind, wealth tier weights, feature toggles for highways, subways, alleys, air and underground tunnels). `hydrology: { type }` optionally selects `lagoon`, `river`, or `sea-coast`; omission means no water and adds no output field.
`streetDesign` supplies numeric road and sidewalk profiles, [src/streets/construction/schema/design.ts](src/streets/construction/schema/design.ts). Omission selects district modules: 1/2/4 lanes at 4/7/14 m, uniform 4.2 m paved sidewalks (4 m panels plus a 0.2 m inner separator), and an additional 0.2 m curb and 0.5 m gutter per side. Selected interior four-lane runs near district centers reserve a separate 3.4 m ornamental median. Explicit source designs retain 2/4/6 m paving and 0.3 m gutters. Explicit street profiles may have 1 or 2 lanes; avenues have 4. Profiles are selected before land subdivision. Saved blueprints retain their authored dimensions.
`pavingDesign` optionally supplies caller-selected finish family IDs, module pitches, joints and district layout overrides, [src/streets/construction/paving/schema.ts](src/streets/construction/paving/schema.ts). It is validated before generation. The city selects finish families from this input; physical dimensions come from the module catalog. Atlas imports no material assets.
`features.trains` is accepted for saved parameters and resolves to `false`.
Building footprints are rectangles on the shared half-metre construction grid, with one hosting policy for use eligibility and final buildability.
Nested parameter objects and arrays are validated at runtime. Unknown district kinds, wealth tiers and feature names, non-boolean toggles, malformed ranges and non-finite numbers fail with `E_INVALID_PARAMS`. Browser parameter imports apply the same validation before changing the form.

CLI: after `npm run build:cli`, `npm run generate -- --seed <seed> --out <file.json> [--size N] [--max-floors N] [--no-highways] [--no-subways] [--no-alleys]` executes the prepared artifact without modifying the Atlas package and writes the blueprint JSON only to the requested path. Exit 1 on AtlasError (code printed), 2 on usage error. `npm run preview` prepares the same CLI before its server starts.

Build: `npm run build` type-checks the package, writes the CLI to `dist/cli.mjs`, the city worker to `dist/city-worker.mjs`, and the browser preview under `dist/preview/`. `build:cli` and `build:cities` prepare their individual entries. Preview startup prepares both Node entries. Each build preserves the other outputs.

Preview hosting mounts the [city catalog API](src/cities/CONTRACT.md) on the same origin in development and production preview. `ATLAS_CITY_DATA_DIR` defaults to `.atlas-cities` relative to the server working directory. Blueprint generation runs in a worker; saved cities persist there. Exterior and interior generation remain separate stages. The browser consumes this API through the [UI contract](src/ui/CONTRACT.md).

Root construction entries:

- `JunctionGround.plan(input)` / `.build(input)` in [JunctionGround.ts](src/streets/JunctionGround.ts) take [JunctionGroundInput](schema/junction-ground.ts): one isolated, flat, straight, axis-aligned through-avenue T, its original source contact, matching Datum roles and runs, explicit bottom, setback and station pitch. All six sides have the same role intervals and levels. They return the retained source ground plan or its snapshot, with distinct roadway, lip, gutter, curb and paved roles. Other junction topologies, bends, unequal side profiles and grade transitions fail with `E_INVARIANT`; insufficient full-width return land or arm length fails with source evidence. This entry does not activate modern city generation.
- Junction returns share cumulative beveled boundaries and original authored support references. Straight handoffs follow the original run's station phase; widths never shrink. Source claims outside the return domain retain their Datum masks, IDs and levels. Junction sources retain contact/group/node/edge/span provenance. One retained partition assigns every positive-area piece; no exported boundary rebuilds ownership. Input order and repeated calls preserve output bytes. Paving, ramps, materials and render encoding remain separate consumers.
- `JunctionGround.fitting(input)` returns [JunctionFittingConstruction](schema/junction-ground.ts): six original-arm/side fields, source IDs, shared run frames, original handoff stations and two corner transition supports. Exact authored-1mm field intents cover each referenced junction role once within its retained owner; they are not replacement ground. Corner supports define infinite lines for continuous transition joints across paving, curb and gutter; their incident run phases need not coincide. Consumers extend layout-width joint strips beyond owner bounds, reserve them first, then fit cells inside the remaining fields. Opposite-frontage fields share one through-run phase with no extra seam. Every remaining piece keeps its exact owner boundary. This pure entry changes no source or ground snapshot; unsupported sources use `E_INVARIANT`.
- `CityConstructionSupport.assert(design)` in [CityConstructionSupport.ts](src/CityConstructionSupport.ts) accepts district profiles with 0.2 m carriageway increments, 4.2 m paving and 0.5 m gutters, or source profiles with whole-metre carriageways, 2/4/6 m paving and 0.3 m gutters. Both require 0.2 m curb width/rise and 0.02 m lip width/height. Unsupported profiles fail before generation with `E_INVALID_PARAMS`, the field, profile ID and `supportedFormat: modules`.
- `GroundCover.plan(input)` takes legacy [GroundCoverInput](src/streets/GroundCoverSchema.ts) (boundary, water, station bays and surface masks) on the authored 1 mm grid. It returns a runtime-only `GroundCoverPlan`: one opaque exact partition, original boundary, coordinate scale, semantic owner IDs/levels and excluded owner IDs. Ordered legacy ownership is water, station bay, roadway, curb, sidewalk, block, open, then open fringe. Empty semantic owners remain listed.
- `GroundCover.snapshot(plan)` returns shared-vertex `GroundSurface[]` after verifying the partition. The numeric view does not replace or consume its exact state. Snapshot metadata must describe every nonexcluded current owner; unknown owners fail. A fitting consumer can refine those retained handles and publish its own final view.
- `GroundCover.build(input)` composes `plan` and `snapshot`. Off-grid input, inconsistent snapshot metadata and partition errors use `E_INVARIANT`. Ground construction imports no paving implementation, and retains no second owner-polygon cache.
- The same entries accept `format: 'source-claims-v1'`, [source schema](schema/ground.ts), with an authored boundary, excluded land, complete source claims and explicit open remainder. Each source has a unique caller-owned ID, absolute bottom/top and geometry-free street-side, roadway, junction or land provenance. Gutter and gutter-lip are distinct surfaces; other paved functional roles retain sidewalk surface. No modern height comes from legacy ground levels.
- Modern `plan` returns [SourceGroundCoverPlan](src/streets/GroundCoverSchema.ts). Modern `snapshot`/`build` returns `{format, sources, ground}`; every ground record references its source and has the same surface/levels. Subdivision retains source IDs. Metadata is copied; source masks are not a published geometry cache.
- Complete source claims must fit the city minus excluded land and have mutually disjoint interiors. Overlapping masks within one claim form that source's union. Distinct claims cannot resolve an overlap by order, even with equal roles or heights. Out-of-domain claims, conflicting ownership, duplicate IDs, invalid provenance/levels and inconsistent source references fail with `E_INVARIANT` and source evidence. Empty claims and boundary-only contact are valid.
- An empty modern claims list establishes the retained land partition with its explicit open owner. A later construction stage can divide those same owner handles through the partition contract and register matching source/owner metadata before snapshot. Complete raw masks can enter a retained subdomain directly; exported numeric boundaries never rebuild ownership. This entry does not construct junction returns or accept unresolved junction overlaps.
- Source-owned snapshots are an additive construction shape, separate from `CityBlueprint`. City, paving and movement consumers require their own format negotiation before using these roles and heights.
- `CityCrossings.plan/validate`, [CityCrossings.ts](src/CityCrossings.ts), takes grid street nodes and edges, final ground and physical obstacles and publishes [crossing records](schema/blueprint.ts). Each eligible junction arm has a 3 m field with whole 0.5 m stripes, complete roadway coverage and sidewalk walking terminals. Crossing setbacks follow each intersecting arm's physical curb/gutter width and its square paved corner (4.2 m per side for district modules). The marking span keeps 5 cm clear of gutters on every street. Curb connectors cover that unmarked road margin and continue to the walking bands. Continuations and motorway arms have no crossings. Optional land exclusions identify source water and complete original blocks removed by water; an arm touching these authored exclusions is ineligible. Each excluded block must have verified water contact. Missing paving outside those exclusions still fails. Validation rebuilds required records, so saved omissions or altered geometry fail with `E_INVARIANT`.

Saved examples live in `samples/`, both at the current blueprint: `city-urbe-tiny.json` (seed `urbe-tiny`, 400 m) and `city-urbe.json` (seed `urbe`, 1 km). A larger example is one CLI run away.

## Out
`CityBlueprint`: [schema/blueprint.ts](schema/blueprint.ts).
- `meta`: schema version, seed, resolved params, bounds, city grid angle, rectangular city boundary polygon, `buildingGrid: {origin: [0,0], angle: gridAngle, spacing: 0.5}`, `lotSizes`, the standard lot catalog, and `blockTemplates`, the block tilings. Grid angle is radians; coordinates and spacing are metres.
- `meta.lotSizes`: the six rectangles every ordinary parcel's lot is cut to, each with `id`, `width` (street frontage), `depth` (back from the street) and `area`: 16x32, 24x32, 24x40, 40x40, 40x56 and 56x56 m. Every dimension is a multiple of 8 m, so rows and row depths pack a block with at most 7 m left over on each axis. One building kit per (type, tier, size) serves the whole city.
- `meta.blockTemplates`: one entry per block size, zone and street tier the city uses, each with a stable `id`, the block `width` and `depth`, its `zone` (the district kind) and its `lots`: offset from the block's minimum corner, width, depth and catalog `sizeId`. Two blocks alike in size, zone and tier carry the same template id and the same lots, so a consumer builds a block once and instances it at every block origin.
- `districts`: kind (downtown, commercial, residential, industrial, mixed), wealth tier, boundary (a rectangle on the city grid, clipped to the city outline), floor cap.
- `streets`: planar graph of nodes and edges; each edge has class (`street` | `road` | `highway` | `alley`), centerline path (straight grid segments between rectangular junction boxes), carriageway width, per-side sidewalk widths, and an `elevationProfile` of `{ distance, level }` knots measured along its own path. `level` remains the flat or maximum height (0 at grade, 8 on a highway deck, [src/levels.ts](src/levels.ts)). Node `connections` group incident edges by their endpoint level, and a vehicle transfers only inside one group, so a grade road does not connect to the highway crossing above it. The graph also carries pedestrian crossings. Each crossing segment names its street edge, connects the two sidewalk walking bands, has a 3 m width, and publishes fitted 0.5 m zebra stripe polygons over the carriageway. Traffic signals have one head per junction arm with the mast that carries it. `planting` is one flat list of street furniture points for the whole city, each with its `edgeId`, its `kind` (`tree` | `pole` | `bin`, a closed set: skip a kind you do not know rather than draw it wrong) and the spacing its stretch runs at. `highwayStructures` has one construction record per maximal highway run with its ordered edge IDs, continuous path, width, level, deck thickness, ramp lengths, matching elevation profile and support columns.
- `architecture`: [schema/architecture.ts](schema/architecture.ts), the movement plan, versioned by `ARCHITECTURE_VERSION`. Per street edge: reserved widths (carriageway and each side), driving lanes with offset, width, direction and centerline, and walking lanes per side. Per node: the legal turn movements, each joining an arriving and a departing lane inside one elevation group. Crossings join the two walking lanes of their arm. A junction with three or more grade motor arms, one of them a road, alternates two signal phases that release its lanes and crossings. Ramps list each climb from a grade node to a highway deck, stretch by stretch. Every id it names exists in `streets`.
- `blocks`: grade-street-bounded rectangles with a curb strip, sidewalk strip polygons, contained parcels and open areas. `boundary` is the block rectangle, kerb line to kerb line. `template` names the `meta.blockTemplates` entry the block is tiled from; it is absent when water, infrastructure or a landmark merge makes the block's lots differ from that tiling. Curb corners at intersections are square.
- `streets.edges[].districtStyle`: `luxury`, `industrial` or `ordinary` from the midpoint district. Industrial takes precedence; rich/high_rich districts use luxury. Each complete block carries one finish: high_rich blue, rich red, industrial yellow, otherwise ordinary. Caller paving families override block finishes. Atlas publishes identifiers; Streets and Materials supply texture and finish.
- `streets.construction.medians`: selected physical avenue islands, [schema](src/streets/layout/medians/schema.ts), with 2 m paving, 0.2 m curb and 0.5 m gutter per side (3.4 m total), square ends and ornament anchors. Median owners reserve their own ground outside traffic lanes.
- `streets.construction`: ordered through-runs with stable `sr` ids and path distances, [src/streets/construction/schema/sections.ts](src/streets/construction/schema/sections.ts). Grade edges publish `crossSection`: exact lane offsets and separate left/right curb, border, furnishing, walking and frontage bands. Scalar widths equal these totals. Highway dimensions retain their structure contract. Crossing `roadway` endpoints describe the marking span separately from unequal pedestrian approaches.
- `streets.construction.planningReservations`: exact edge-local roadway, left/right sidewalk and walking polygons with their directed sweep model, [src/streets/construction/corridors/CONTRACT.md](src/streets/construction/corridors/CONTRACT.md). A straight corridor ends in a square cap of plain segments, reaching the same distance past its endpoint as the quarter circle inscribed in them.
  Only a bend adds a fan, at a step no finer than 15 degrees. Final ground polygons apply junction, elevation and water ownership; a reservation is not an alternate render or collision surface.
- `streets.construction.waterExcludedBlocks`: original source owner IDs and complete boundaries removed by water before retained blocks are renumbered. Crossing eligibility uses these records with the saved hydrology surfaces.
- `streets.construction.reservations`, [handoff](src/streets/layout/reservations/CONTRACT.md), publishes original frontage/corner supports, exact saved ground indices, local parcel exclusions, source-native parking and non-owning station/highway references. New generation includes version 1.0.0; saved blueprints without it retain their existing construction format.
- `streets.construction.modules`, [module schema](src/streets/construction/modules/schema.ts), publishes reusable physical prism definitions and repeated quarter-turn placements. These prisms own street rendering and collision, including panel joints, gutters, guardrails and parking returns. Ground records marked `moduleBlockId` are compact planning covers. Optional frontage owners describe the outer sidewalk ring, which stops at any shoreline reaching the city boundary. Saved fitted paving uses its [own contract](src/streets/construction/paving/CONTRACT.md).
- `parcels`: type (residential, hotel, offices, corpo, hospital, clinic, police, military, factory, commerce, mall, restaurant, coffee_shop, park), tier (poor, mid, rich, high_rich), lot polygon, footprint polygon, street access point and 3D envelope (min/max floors, nominal floor height and max height; real per-floor elevations are owned by Exterior). A footprint is a complete grid-aligned rectangle inside its setback land. It satisfies the type's minimum band and core fit; unused land remains part of its lot.
- A `park` parcel is public green: a standard lot that carries no building, published with its lot, tier, street access and `lotSize`, and with no `footprint` and no `envelope`. It is the only parcel type without them, and it carries no entry in `volumetric.buildings`. Every other parcel allows at least two floors (`maxFloors >= 2`, `maxHeight >= 9.0 m`).
- Every lot of one template slot carries the same envelope band. The first block to use a template fixes each slot's `minFloors` and `maxFloors` from its own district, and every later block of that template publishes the same band there, so a district's skyline comes from the templates it uses. Floor pitch stays per type, so `floorHeight` and `maxHeight` follow each parcel's own use. A lot outside any template keeps the band its zoning drew, and a parcel whose hosted core cannot carry the band publishes what the core allows.
- A parcel is either ordinary or a landmark. An ordinary parcel carries `lotSize`, the `meta.lotSizes` id its lot is exactly, in either orientation. A landmark parcel carries `landmark: true` and no `lotSize`: it is one of a kind on its own plot and is built bespoke. Floor counts are per parcel either way; the `landmarkFloors` parameter selects exact tower heights and is independent of this flag.
- `transit`: connected subway lines at -12 m with 6 m tunnel width, 140 x 8 m platforms and 5 m headroom. Stations publish street entrances, shafts and continuous 3D `accessPaths` through stairs and level passages. `entranceBays` reserve complete directed-side land and walking approaches, [src/transit/reservations/schema.ts](src/transit/reservations/schema.ts). `subwayDemand` records the pre-parcel population forecast and line target. Lines end inside their terminal platforms. `busStops`, `busRoutes`, `trainStations` and `trainLines` are empty saved-format compatibility collections.
- `hydrology` (optional): [src/hydro/schema/hydrology-plan.schema.json](src/hydro/schema/hydrology-plan.schema.json). It publishes independent `seedId`, water surfaces, shoreline paths and water-side construction bands, elevation, depth, material key and bridge/tunnel contacts. A structure's optional `corridor` is its exact wet-contact reservation; `path` locates that contact along the source network and `width` remains metadata. The source network owns the complete deck or tunnel geometry. Renderers consume water `surfaces` at `elevation`, shade to `depth`, resolve `materialKey`, and draw shoreline `path` and `band`.
- `volumetric`: one prism per parcel plus disjoint planning ground (roadway, curb, gutter, sidewalk, block, open). Module-city levels are roadway -0.2..0 m, gutter -0.03..0 m, curb -0.03..0.2 m, and sidewalk/block/open 0..0.2 m. Physical module prisms retain their exact joints and lip heights. Water is published separately.
- `stats`: population estimate, parcel counts per type, per district.
- `report.degraded`: every element published in its simplest valid form instead of failing the plan, each with its `id`, its `kind` (`junction-box`, `crossing`, `corridor`, `lot`, `station`) and the `reason` in plain words. Empty on a clean plan.

## Errors
Closed set, thrown as `AtlasError { code, message, details? }` ([schema/blueprint.ts](schema/blueprint.ts)):
- `E_INVALID_PARAMS`: params fail validation; message names the field.
- `E_UNSATISFIABLE`: params cannot yield a coherent city (e.g. size too small for the district count, or below 420 m on one axis when hydrology is requested).
- `E_INVARIANT`: post-generation coherence check failed; atlas bug, report with seed and params. One element that fails its own check never raises it: the element is published in its simplest valid form and listed in `report.degraded`. `E_INVARIANT` is left for a plan with no valid form at all.

## Invariants
- Rectangular hosting maximizes whole-cell area, then short-side width, then lower grid coordinates and wider U dimension. Full transformed lattice coordinates preserve each building corner; analytic containment checks the complete rectangle against its setback land, allowing only floating-point transform roundoff. Ineligible lots use the existing lighter use, neighbor merge or open-land policy; no rectangle is clipped into an irregular building.

- Street intersections are shared row/column identities. Each row and column has a fixed road profile. Blocks are rectangles sized in whole 8 m modules, the module the lot catalog is cut on, and one axis carries at most two block sizes, so a city is a few block sizes repeated. Blocks run about 120 m and grow with the square root of the city beyond a kilometre: the default 3 km city runs 200 m blocks. Seed variation changes which blocks take the longer size, and the profile of each row and column.
- Every ring the blueprint publishes as land or ground is an axis-aligned rectangle with four corners: the city boundary, districts, blocks, their kerb, sidewalk and open areas, lots, footprints, ground regions and building volumes. Street edges are straight segments between rectangular junction boxes. A city with `hydrology` is the exception: water and the land it cuts follow the shoreline. Variety comes from zones, block templates and floors per lot, never from geometry. Alleys remain empty.
- The [street layout](src/streets/layout/CONTRACT.md) reserves lane widths, complete block panels and outer sidewalks directly within the requested extent. Building parcels use the remaining block interiors.
- Districts use axis-aligned cuts between planned centers and remain rectangular within the city boundary.
- Levels are the one place height lives for networks: a consumer reads street height by linearly interpolating its ordered elevation profile, and stacks flat tracks and platforms from `level`, never from class. Every street profile starts at distance 0, ends at the exact 2D path length, has strictly increasing distances and stays within 0 and its declared maximum level. Every non-highway profile is flat. Node connection groups contain every incident edge exactly once and only join equal endpoint levels.
- With highways enabled, a seeded interior row or column reserves four lanes before blocks are sized. Its continuous highway crosses the urban grid with blocks on both sides. Its termini stay within 30 m of the boundary and its length accommodates both ramps and a supported flat span.
- Every highway edge belongs to exactly one `streets.highwayStructures` record. Its ramps rise linearly from grade to the 8 m deck over 60 m only at city-edge termini, shortened only when the whole run is shorter. The structure and its routing edges publish the same height knots, including a breakpoint when a ramp ends inside an edge. Its 2 x 2 m supports stand under the flat deck at no more than 30 m intervals, from ground to the deck underside. A grade street passing beneath cuts the deck into clear stretches: one column stands at each end of every stretch, so a crossing is carried from both of its edges, and the columns inside a stretch are spread evenly, never closer than one support width. A crossing wider than 28 m admits no such pair within the pitch and fails with `E_INVARIANT`; the widest the district modules build is the 27.6 m avenue with its median, curbs and underpass sidewalks. Decks keep 1 m of construction clearance beyond each edge from every building footprint. Support footprints never enter a building footprint, subway entrance shaft, crossing roadway, curb or sidewalk. All support centers are on the 1 mm coordinate grid.
- Highway construction supports an early envelope stage and later support placement, [src/streets/construction/highway/CONTRACT.md](src/streets/construction/highway/CONTRACT.md). The edge-only envelope contains every final structure field except supports. Support completion preserves that envelope exactly; the combined entry point remains available. Assigned routing profiles are checked against the shared run height function.
- One element never kills a plan. A junction box whose arm has no clear grade carriageway is dropped, a crossing whose landings are incomplete keeps its box and loses its markings, a lot that carries no two-floor building becomes a park, and a subway that cannot reserve its platforms or entrances leaves the city without one. Each is listed in `report.degraded` with its id and reason.
- The clear street between two junction boxes is a whole multiple of 2 m, so street construction closes every run with its 8, 4 and 2 m pieces. A junction approach `field` is the box boundary on its arm: it clears every other arm of the junction, then steps out to the 2 m grid measured from the edge start. An end with no box leaves the edge's own end on that grid. A street with no box at either end is on the grid only when its own length is, and the few that are not (both junctions standing on water) are listed in `report.degraded` as corridors.
- Every standard lot's envelope allows at least two floors: `maxFloors >= 2` and `maxHeight >= 9.0 m`, the base and crown a building kit stacks. The minimum holds unless the district's own floor cap is 1; a lot that cannot reach it, including one hosting no core rectangle, is published as a park with no envelope.
- IDs are globally unique across the whole blueprint (disjoint prefixes per collection).
- Street graph is connected; every parcel's access point lies on a sidewalk of its access edge.
- Every edge is a real run of street: its two nodes differ, no path point repeats, and no vertex turns more than 120 degrees. A sharper turn would fold the edge's sidewalk band back over its own roadway.
- A physical street path appears once per source family, independent of direction or collinear subdivisions on the coordinate grid. Street and road traces share a family with road precedence; highways and pedestrian alleys have separate families.
- Initial graph normalization resolves same-endpoint, same-family paths at the configured node snap radius. Both paths must advance strictly along their common endpoint chord and stay within that radius at every shared projected station. Returning paths and separated parallels remain distinct. Canonical order is class precedence, shorter length, then whole-path grid key; each removed path matches a retained representative directly. Extension uses exact identity only.
- Initial graph simplification retains every source endpoint shared with another source path as a required anchor. Only exact shared coordinates establish these authored joins.
- Sidewalks connect through complete external crossing approaches, [construction contract](src/streets/crossings/CONTRACT.md). Original node connection groups retain their identity inside each physical junction. Positive-width external grade arms with both sidewalks publish one field each; pedestrian alleys and highways publish none. Whole 0.5 m stripes repeat inside a 3 m field with equal margins. Crossing-only connectors can include the unmarked road margin; terminal strips must fit actual pedestrian ground and their declared walking bands. Complete fields avoid intersecting traffic and physical obstacles under the caller's pedestrian-clearance setting. Derived cuts retain their shared source-edge interpolation coordinates.
- Traffic signals use each physical junction's external approach cuts. A junction with three or more grade motor arms, including at least one road, carries one head per arm, or none. Highways and pedestrian alleys carry no head. A pole stands in its approach's furnishing band (right-hand traffic); `facing` looks back down the arm and `mast` reaches square to it across the approach lanes to the roadway centerline. Heads stand at the exterior boundary of the complete crossing field.
- Street furniture stands in the furnishing band on finished sidewalk land, clear of parking and guardrails. Selected frontages hold one or two trees at 8 m stations in downtown/commercial districts and 12 m elsewhere; other frontages remain unplanted. Lighting uses every third station independently. Furniture and short guardrail groups keep 6 m clear of crossings, station entrances and parcel access points. Highways and alleys carry no planting. Placement: [CityFurniture.ts](src/CityFurniture.ts), [Planting.ts](src/streets/Planting.ts).
- Every subway station belongs to a line serving at least two stations. The network is connected and contains no immediate double-back. Line endpoints lie inside terminal platforms; an insufficient route or unavailable entrance land leaves the city without a subway, listed in `report.degraded` as the `station` element. Surface transit compatibility collections are always empty.
- Subway entrance bays reserve land before parcels. Each shaft is 8 by 3 m with a 1 m apron outside the complete sidewalk. Its approach starts in the authored walking band, whose width remains clear. Bay footprints receive sidewalk paving and exclude parcel lots. The early subway plan uses the zoning population forecast and survives final population changes unchanged. Shafts stay clear of highway supports.
- A station is walkable end to end: its platform is a simple ring covering the station's position; underground it carries one shaft and one access path per entrance. The stair centerline descends through four non-vertical switchback flights inside its shaft, its passage stays at platform level, every leg joins exactly, and the final handoff lies inside the platform. An underground entrance is at most 30 m from its platform: NFPA 130 caps the walk from the platform's most remote point to the street at 100 m, and half a 140 m platform takes 70 of them. Platform sizes are researched ([src/transit/stations.ts](src/transit/stations.ts)): 140 x 8 m subway island.
- Parcels never overlap; parcels, curb, sidewalk and open areas cover their block rectangle; ground surfaces cover the city without gaps.
- Module support covers are constructed once per template and placed by their shared transforms. Road rectangles, module covers, lots, station paving and open land cover the city without positive-area overlap. Station additions exclude existing pavement and their land is subtracted from lots and open areas.
- District blocks repeat one 2 x 2 m inner panel, four 1 x 1 m outer panels and a 0.2 m inner separator around all four sides. Paving is 4.2 m wide; curb width/rise is 0.2 m and gutter width is 0.5 m. Source profiles retain 2/4/6 m paving and 0.3 m gutters. Both formats use 12 mm panel joints, 0.02 m gutter lips and square corners one sidewalk wide. Side reservations add curb and gutter outside the paving.
- Block and ground polygons are simple rings with positive area. Every street parks on one kerb: a block reserves a bay on its south and west frontages, so each street is parked on one side and clear on the other. A highway frontage and a divided avenue carry none. A bay holds up to six slots, as many as the zone gives (residential and mixed 6, industrial 5, commercial 4, downtown 3) and the frontage fits, centred on the frontage with its stations on the 2 m grid street construction closes a run on. District parking uses 6 x 2 m slots in a rectangular bay with 2 m of support at each end, and preserves the inner 2.2 m walking band, including its separator. Source parking uses 6 x 2.5 m slots on 6 m paving with a 3.5 m rear walking strip. A bay ends in a 45 degree return over its 2 m end run: its footprint keeps the full length at the kerb and pulls the back line in one end run at each end, 40 m2 for three district slots. Every bay stands in the roadway ground record its module notched into the kerb, one record per bay, checked on every plan. Bays stay clear of highways and corners. Guardrails use repeated 2 m modules with corner and parking clearances.
- Feature toggles are respected: a disabled feature produces no entities of that kind.
- Hydrology is derived from its independent seed stream before infrastructure placement. Water surfaces and shorelines are bounded, snapped to 1 mm, CCW, simple and byte-stable. Parcels, accesses, stations, entrances and land cover avoid water. Water reaching the city boundary stops the outer sidewalk ring at its shoreline: a ring module standing in water is not placed, a side cut this way publishes one frontage per remaining stretch, and the vacated land becomes open ground. A network touches water only inside its typed bridge or tunnel contact; constructed street contacts include both actual sidewalk widths. Highway supports in water belong to a published highway bridge.
- Every parcel footprint hosts the core rectangle its type needs, derived from interior's published core feasibility ([src/zoning/core.ts](src/zoning/core.ts) mirrors the constants of `../interior/schemas/core-feasibility.json`; a test fails when that file moves). A rectangle is a core mode's band length by its plate depth, with the stair shaft sized for the longest flight the recipe allows, plus one 0.5 m snap (the corridor face and the core start land on interior's grid) and twice the deepest facade (0.24 m) on both axes; it fits in either orientation. Walkup 10.38 x 7.58 m, walkup with two stairs 16.88 x 7.58 m, compact elevator core 12.38 x 11.58 m, standard elevator core 19.38 x 7.58 m. A heavy type (offices, corpo, hotel, hospital, mall, factory) hosts the compact rectangle; a light type (residential, commerce, restaurant, coffee_shop, clinic, police, military) hosts the walkup rectangle, and one of the two-stair rectangles when its footprint exceeds 460 m2.
- Blocks are subdivided into standard lots, [src/blocks/StandardLots.ts](src/blocks/StandardLots.ts). The tiler is keyed by block width, depth, zone and whether the street is rich, never by block id or position: a block's buildable land carries rows running along its longer side, row depths are catalog depths chosen to fill the block across, and each row is filled with catalog widths drawn from the zone's weights. Land no standard lot covers, including the courtyard strip a deep block keeps, is open area. Water and infrastructure cuts drop any lot that would leave the buildable land rather than reshaping it; that block then publishes no template. Industrial districts take only the four larger sizes.
- A rich or high_rich parcel's lot is at least 24 m on its short side, three 8 m bays: a block in a rich or high_rich district cuts only lots that wide, and a block too small to cut one keeps the whole catalog and its parcels step down to mid. A parcel drawn rich on a narrower lot anywhere else steps down the same way, so no rich family is asked to fit two bays. Mid, poor and every lower tier keep the 16 m lots.
- Landmark lots are the exception to the catalog: 10 to 30 blocks per city each merge a run of two or three neighbouring lots into one plot, the largest first so the city's anchors (hospital, police, military, mall) and singular towers have somewhere to stand. A city with fewer than 40 blocks gets one landmark per four blocks.
- Every parcel footprint keeps its type's band end to end, the short side of its rectangle: `HEAVY_BAND` 11.58 m, `LIGHT_BAND` 7.58 m ([src/zoning/bands.ts](src/zoning/bands.ts)). The band is the width between the footprint's two long sides along the whole footprint, where an oblique end cut is a cap and not a narrowing. The zoner assigns a lot only types whose band it hosts; a heavy type whose footprint cannot host its rectangle is retyped to the district's main light type (commerce in downtown, commercial and industrial districts, residential in residential and mixed ones); a lot hosting no rectangle becomes a park, keeping its lot and its standard size.
- Envelope floors stay within what the hosted core allows: no cap with an elevator rectangle, 6 floors with the two-stair walkup rectangle, 4 with the walkup rectangle alone. That cap is the one thing that moves a lot off its template slot's band.
- Every generated envelope allocates each floor at the greater of its hard family minimum and Exterior's mirrored generation policy: 4 m clear height plus 0.5 m allowance. Nominal pitch is at least 4.5 m; taller type programs retain their pitch. Maximum height allocates every permitted floor at the nominal pitch, rounded to centimetres. Local [floor policy](src/zoning/floorMinimums.ts) mirrors [Exterior constants](../exterior/schemas/floor-constants.json) without runtime sibling imports.
